# The World Bank

Twelve visual traditions to draw from. Pick ONE. Commit. Do not mix.

## 1. Swiss / International Typographic

Era: 1950s-1970s, Basel and Zurich.
Voice: Objective, systematic, confident.
Palette: Black, white, one red or one ochre.
Type: Helvetica-adjacent grotesque, flush-left, ragged-right.
Composition: Grid-first. Asymmetry by math, not by chance.
Use for: Data-heavy, authoritative, technical. Dashboards, editorial, documentation.
Reference: Swiss Federal Railways, Helvetica documentary, Braun.

## 2. Bauhaus

Era: 1919-1933, Weimar, Dessau, Berlin.
Voice: Constructivist, pedagogical, primary.
Palette: Red, yellow, blue, black, off-white.
Type: Geometric sans. Futura or Bauhaus-adjacent.
Composition: Triangles, circles, primaries as structure.
Use for: Educational, manifestos, cultural institutions.
Reference: Herbert Bayer, Josef Albers, Laszlo Moholy-Nagy.

## 3. Editorial / Broadsheet

Voice: Authoritative, human, considered.
Palette: Cream paper, ink black, one accent (burgundy, forest).
Type: Serif headlines, sans body.
Composition: Multi-column, drop caps, rules as dividers.
Use for: Long-form, magazines, journals, personal blogs.
Reference: NYT Magazine, A List Apart, Are.na blog.

## 4. Riso / Zine

Era: 1980s onward, DIY publishing.
Voice: Raw brands, energetic, democratic.
Palette: 2-3 spot colors (fluorescent pink, ris.
o blue, fluorescent orange) on off-white.
Type: Mix of high-contrast serif and monospace.
Composition: Misregistration, ink bleed, textureReference.
Use for: Music, art, community, zines, indie products.
Reference: Perfectly Imperfect, Hato Press.

## 5. Japanese: Editorial

Era: Post-war Japanese graphic design.
Voice: Quiet, spacious, deliberate.
Palette: Muted earth, single seasonal accent.
Type: Mix of Japanese mincho (serif) and a Latin sans with restrained weight.
Composition: Vertical rhythm, negative space as content.
Use for: Fashion, galleries, contemplative products.
Reference: Kenya Hara, MUJI, Idea Magazine.

## 6. Dutch Experimental

Era: De Stijl onward, Wim Crouwel, Studio Dumbar.
Voice: Grid-breaking, mathematical, energetic.
Palette: Restrained with one saturated hit.
Type: New Alphabet, Gridnik, hard grotesques.
Composition: Asymmetry, overlap, arithmetic tension.
Use for: Cultural institutions, identity work, portfolio.
Reference: Wim Crouwel, Experimental Jetset.

## 7. Mid-Century Signage

Era: 1950s-1960s America and Europe.
Voice: Optimistic, geometric, crafted.
Palette: Gold, teal, brick, cream.
Type: Geometric serif or thick sans.
Composition: Arcs, streamlined curves, dots.
Use for: Hospitality, restaurants, retro Saul Bass, Paul Rand, Container Corporation.

## 8. Technical / Lab Manual

Era: Post-war technical publishing.
Voice: Precise, functional, instructional.
Palette: Off-white paper, ink, one safety accent.
Type: Monospace plus a clinical sans.
Composition: Numbered callouts, exploded diagrams, ruled tables.
Use for: Developer tools, hardware, scientific software.
Reference: Bell Labs technical papers, NASA manuals.

## 9. Museum Exhibition Label

Era: Contemporary gallery signage.
Voice: Curatorial, minimal, exact.
Palette: White wall, ink black, small accent.
Type: Small caps, tracked, with a serif body.
Composition: Hairline dividers, numbered sections, tight type.
Use for: Portfolio, catalog, editorial micro-site.
Reference: MoMA wall labels, Tate exhibition graphics.

## 10. Art Deco / Grand Hotel

Era: 1925-1940.
Voice: Luxurious, geometric, vertical.
Palette: Deep green, gold, black, cream.
Type: Decorative geometric caps.
Composition: Vertical sunbursts, arcs, symmetric.
Use for: Premium hospitality, luxury, ceremony.
Reference: Cassandre posters, Chrysler Building signage.

## 11. Scandinavian Functionalism

Era: 1930s onward.
Voice: Honest, warm, functional.
Palette: Unbleached paper, muted blue, red, green, natural wood tones.
Type: Humanist sans (Futura, Avenir) and restrained serif.
Composition: Grid with breathing room, tactile materials.
Use for: Furniture, home, sustainable products, public services.
Reference: Arne Jacobsen, Bang and Olufsen.

## 12. 90s Japanese Web / Y2K

Era: 1996-2003, revived as aesthetic.
Voice: Playful, chaotic, energetic.
Palette: Hot pink, cyan, chrome, black.
Type: Mix of pixel, gothic Japanese, and heavy Latin.
Composition: Tables, borders, marquee energy.
Use for: Nostalgic brands, music, communities, intentional retro.
Reference: GeoCities, early Chiebukuro, Y2K design revivals.

## How to Choose

1. Ask: what world would the SUBJECT live in? A coffee roaster: mid-century signage. A database tool: Swiss. A gallery: Japanese editorial.
2. Ask: which world is the OPPOSITE of the category default? If every SaaS is Swiss-minimal, try Riso. If every gallery is minimal, try Dutch experimental.
3. Commit fully. A half-Swiss half-Bauhaus page is worse than a full-Swiss page. The world is a lens, not a mood board.
